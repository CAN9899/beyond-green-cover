Beyond Green Cover

Street-level tree mapping as a tool for climate-responsive urbanism in Aligarh, India.

An institutional street corridor at Aligarh Muslim University offers a case for asking what a planting inventory can reveal beyond satellite-derived vegetation cover. The study relates urban heat, street ecology, everyday movement and the production of local ecological knowledge to questions of climate-responsive urbanism and landscape management.

04 / Research approach

Reading urban green infrastructure at street scale

The analytical question examines how a planted street corridor can be read as part of urbanism rather than as green cover alone. This study uses a secondary-city campus corridor to consider the information gained, and lost, when vegetation is represented at different scales and disconnected from the lived street environment.

01 / The crisis

Land-cover change and urban heat

Khan, Bano and Khan (2025) report an increase of 21.10% in built-up area and a decline of 24.55% in vegetation area across Aligarh between 2001 and 2021. Their satellite-based analysis also finds systematically higher land-surface temperatures in the wider main city than the AMU campus area. These findings establish the wider spatial context, not a temperature measurement for this corridor.

Khan, Bano & Khan (2025)

02 / The knowledge gap

What the overhead view cannot tell us

Satellite land-cover and LST analysis describes broad patterns but does not, at ordinary classification resolution, provide a verified inventory of roadside taxa, tree dimensions, planting conditions or the experience of walking through particular street edges. A plan-derived register can organise these questions; direct fieldwork is required to answer them.

Gupta et al. (2024) / Ocón et al. (2024)

03 / The method

Plan to register, register to questions

The present prototype organises a six-zone landscape drawing into a taxonomic register, five editable tally segments and a schematic map. It computes tree density, proportional abundance, Shannon diversity and idealised crown area. The final two spatial zones are aggregated in the tally. The map symbols are not georeferenced tree records.

Density = stems / segment length × 100
Shannon H' = −Σ pᵢ ln(pᵢ)
Crown footprint = Σ nᵢ π(dᵢ / 2)²

04 / Urban stewardship

Towards a shared campus tree record

A validated register could give horticulture staff, students and researchers a common basis for checking species names, recording canopy conditions and discussing planting priorities. In urbanistic terms, the value lies in linking planting structure to shade, walkability, edge conditions and long-term landscape care. Such joint stewardship is a proposed application, not a collaboration that the prototype claims has already occurred.

Vailshery, Jaganmohan & Nagendra (2013)

Evidence boundary

Current: a plan-derived working register, exploratory stem counts, explicit formulas and an illustrative map.

Not yet established: individual-tree positional accuracy, measured canopy continuity, pedestrian thermal comfort, statistically sampled uncertainty or a demonstrated cooling effect.

Field validation protocol

  1. Check the original drawing schedule and resolve species identifications in the field.
  2. Record tree coordinates, crown diameters, DBH and condition consistently.
  3. Survey both walking edges and drainage constraints; verify locations of planting gaps.
  4. Measure shade and thermal conditions before drawing conclusions about cooling or interventions.
